\subsection{Anchor-balanced multi-positive alignment}
\label{sec:alignment-objective}
\label{sec:paper-v4-phase1}
We first align the encoders with a decoupled multi-positive objective,
accounting for the many-to-many structure of enzyme--reaction associations.
For anchor $a$, $P_B(a)$ contains all known training neighbors among the
batch's distinct opposite-modality endpoints; $N_B(a)$ contains the remainder.
With $s_{ax}^{(1)}=\mathbf{b}_a^\top\mathbf{b}_x/\tau_1$ and $\tau_1=0.2$,
we minimize
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{align}}^{(1)}=
 \frac12\sum_{X\in\{R,E\}}\frac{1}{|\mathcal A_X|}\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_X}
 \frac{1}{|P_B(a)|}\sum_{p\in P_B(a)}
 \log\!\left(1+\sum_{n\in N_B(a)}e^{s_{an}^{(1)}-s_{ap}^{(1)}}\right),
 \label{eq:paper-phase1}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal A_X$ contains type-$X$ anchors with nonempty positive and
negative sets. Each positive competes only with $N_B(a)$, excluding other
known positives from its denominator. Normalizing by $|P_B(a)|$ prevents
anchors with more positives from receiving greater weight; the two
directional losses are averaged equally. The implemented phase-1 objective
also includes entropy and diversity penalties on residue attention
(Appendix~\ref{sec:v4-phase1}).

\subsection{Full-graph refinement}
\label{sec:paper-v4-phase2}
After initial alignment, we freeze both encoders and refine their
representations over the complete training association graph.
Two residual heads, $h_E$ and $h_R$, learn additive adjustments to enzyme
and reaction embeddings. For endpoint $a$ of modality $X\in\{R,E\}$,
the refined representation is
$\mathbf{u}_a^{\mathrm{tr}}=\vunit{\mathbf{b}_a+\kappa h_X(\mathbf{b}_a)}$,
where $\kappa=0.2$ controls the residual contribution.
We optimize the heads using cross-entropy with a uniform target
distribution over each query's known positive partners:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{CE}}^{(2)}=
 \frac12\sum_{X\in\{R,E\}}\frac{1}{|\mathcal T_X|}\sum_{a\in\mathcal T_X}
 \left[\log\sum_{x\in\mathcal T_{\bar X}}e^{s_{ax}^{(2)}}
       -\frac{1}{|P(a)|}\sum_{p\in P(a)}s_{ap}^{(2)}\right],
 \label{eq:paper-phase2-ce}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal T_R=\vR$, $\mathcal T_E=\vE$, $\bar X$ denotes the opposite
modality, and $P(a)$ contains the training positives of $a$.
Scores are $s_{ax}^{(2)}=(\mathbf{u}_a^{\mathrm{tr}})^\top
\mathbf{u}_x^{\mathrm{tr}}/\tau_2$, with $\tau_2=0.2$.
Each query is compared with every opposite-modality training endpoint,
including its positive partners. An identity penalty $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{id}}$
discourages cosine displacement from the base embeddings, averaged equally
over reactions and enzymes. The complete refinement objective is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L^{(2)}=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CE}}^{(2)}+10\mathcal L_{\mathrm{id}}.
 \label{eq:paper-phase2-total}
\end{equation}
Only the residual heads are updated; both endpoint representations remain
independently computable. Appendix~\ref{sec:v4-phase2} gives the head
architecture and identity-penalty definition.

\subsection{Bidirectional scoring}
\label{sec:paper-v4-retrieval}
A dictionary indexes training reactions. Frozen features are normalized,
centered on training endpoints, and renormalized. Reaction coordinates
$\mathbf{a}_r$ are normalized exponential cosine similarities to training reactions.
For each enzyme, we find the 32 nearest training proteins; each reaction
coordinate takes the maximum exponential similarity among linked neighbors.
Normalization gives $\mathbf{a}_e$.
Both kernels use temperature 0.03 (Appendix~\ref{sec:v4-dictionary}).

For retrieval, we increase the fused protein contribution to
$\tilde{\mathbf{b}}_e=\vunit{\mathbf{g}_e+3s\mathbf{f}_e}$ and halve the residual
coefficient, obtaining
$\mathbf{u}_e=\vunit{\tilde{\mathbf{b}}_e+0.1h_E(\tilde{\mathbf{b}}_e)}$ and
$\mathbf{u}_r=\vunit{\mathbf{b}_r+0.1h_R(\mathbf{b}_r)}$.
These adjustments follow training on the native $\mathbf{b}_e,\mathbf{b}_r$.
For either endpoint $x$, the complete representation and score are
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf{z}_x=[\sqrt{0.6}\,\mathbf{u}_x;\sqrt{0.4}\,\mathbf{a}_x],\qquad
 S(r,e)=\mathbf{z}_r^\top\mathbf{z}_e
 =0.6\,\mathbf{u}_r^\top\mathbf{u}_e+0.4\,\mathbf{a}_r^\top\mathbf{a}_e.
 \label{eq:paper-score}
\end{equation}
The $512+|\vR|$ dimensional vectors support both directions without
direction-specific heads or reranking. Each target training split has
independently fitted retrieval weights and dictionary statistics
(Appendix~\ref{app:v4-recipe}).
